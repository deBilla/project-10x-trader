Autonomous trading agent · paper only

An LLM trading bot that measured itself, then fired itself

Project 10X started as a Claude-driven intraday day-trading agent on Alpaca. Then it got a backtester, and the backtester said the strategy lost money. What survived the measurement is a deterministic trend engine with the language model demoted to a single, narrow job — and the machinery that proved which parts were worth keeping.

What this is

A single-user, single-process trading agent that runs against an Alpaca paper account. It holds a watchlist of 30 diversified large-caps and ETFs, computes a daily trend signal in plain Python, asks Claude to read the news before it buys, and enforces position and drawdown limits in code that no model output can reach.

It is roughly 3,100 lines of Python across 22 modules, with 67 tests that need no network. Two offline harnesses exist purely to measure things before they go live: a backtester for the strategy, and a counterfactual eval for the language model.

The point

The interesting artefact here is not the bot. It is the pair of measurement harnesses that decided what the bot became — and the fact that both of them returned answers their author did not want.

The three-act arc

01

Build the obvious thing killed

A ReAct loop: feed Claude precomputed indicators for each symbol every few minutes, let it reason, let it call Alpaca order tools through MCP, gate every order with an in-process risk hook. Mean-reversion on RSI and Bollinger bands. It worked, in the sense that it placed orders and never breached a limit.

Verdict plumbing sound · strategy unmeasured
02

Build the backtester the pivot

No-lookahead bar simulation, intrabar stops, modelled slippage. It reported the mean-reversion strategy at −0.08% expectancy per trade, profit factor 0.45, and about −50% on the active variant. Net of a modelled 10 bps round-turn cost, the gross edge was roughly +2 bps — a rounding error against its own transaction costs.

The same harness found daily Donchian trend-following at +5.8% expectancy and profit factor near 3. The live engine was rewritten the same day to remove the model from the trade decision entirely.

Verdict strategy replaced · LLM removed from decisions
03

Measure the model itself shipped

Claude came back in one narrow role: read recent headlines before an entry and block the trade if the news is strongly bearish or earnings are imminent. It can block, never initiate. Because news sentiment is not backtestable as a signal, a second harness measures it as a cost — replaying history twice through the same simulator, once with the veto and once without.

The answer, honestly reported: approximately free and approximately useless. +0.03 percentage points of expectancy, block precision 63.6% against a 45% random baseline — above it, but at n=11 the odds of doing that well by chance are about 0.17. Suggestive and underpowered.

Verdict kept, constrained, and not expanded

Before you read the numbers

Every figure on this site comes from a paper account and an offline simulator. The trend edge was measured on mega-cap survivors across a broadly bullish multi-year window, which is the most flattering possible sample. The news-filter eval is contaminated by the model's own hindsight, and says so on every run.

None of this is evidence that the system makes money. It is evidence about which parts of it were worth building, which is a smaller and more defensible claim.

Project 10X · paper trading only, no real capital · this is an engineering write-up, not financial advice. Automated trading can lose money quickly; the risk layer exists precisely because the strategy might be wrong.
