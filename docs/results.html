Project 10X Overview Approach Design Evals Results

04 — Results

What was actually achieved

Two things: a trading system with a measured positive expectancy on paper, and — more durably — a pair of harnesses that can tell whether any future change to it is an improvement. The second is the one worth having.

The scoreboard

The 12-symbol and 30-symbol trend numbers differ because the universe changed, not because the strategy did. Broadening from 12 to 30 names lowered per-trade expectancy from 7.7% to 5.5% while raising Sharpe from ~1.8 to 2.45 and holding max drawdown near 9–12%. That is diversification doing exactly what it is supposed to do: less return per bet, more return per unit of risk.

It also partly answers the survivorship objection. The re-test held up including its losers — TSLA, UNH and V all lost money in the window and the portfolio edge survived them.

This is the canonical trend-following profile and it is worth reading carefully, because it explains every design choice upstream of it. The win rate is barely better than a coin flip. The edge lives entirely in the 2.2:1 ratio between average win and average loss — which exists only because there is no take-profit and the exit is a trailing channel.

Two implications follow. First, capping winners would destroy the edge, which is why the strategy has no profit target at all. Second, a system like this trades rarely — 255 trades across 30 symbols over roughly four years is about two per symbol per year. Activity is not the goal, and the long stretches where the journal records nothing are the strategy working.

Why the 20% catastrophic stop almost never fires

Average loss is 7.2% against a 20% stop. The trailing channel exit is nearly always faster, which means the wide stop is a backstop against an overnight gap rather than a strategy parameter. That is deliberate — a tight stop on a multi-week trend system just converts winners into losers via noise.

The live forward result

Everything above this point is a backtest. In September 2026 the obvious question finally got asked — what has the agent actually done? — and the answer is the most valuable data in the project, because it is the only part nobody could have fitted.

The agent had been running continuously for three months on a $1,000 paper account: 481 ticks, 23 buys, 21 sells, 16 news vetoes, fully deployed in 10 positions.

Underperformed a passive basket of its own universe by 4.9 percentage points, and SPY by 3.9. Against a backtest implying roughly 39%/yr, this is the gap that matters — and it is not a subtle one.

The cause was an implementation bug, not the strategy

The live trade log did not look like the backtest's. Three signals, all pointing the same way:

  • Same-day round trips. SPY ran S07-29 B07-29 S07-30 B08-04 S09-01 B09-09 S09-10. Sold and re-bought within one session. Same pattern on AAPL, AMD, QQQ and LLY.
  • Every exit was intraday. All 21 landed between 13:00 and 18:00 UTC. The US market closes at 20:00. Not one exit happened after a close.
  • Trade rate ~2.75× the backtest. 44 order actions in 93 days against the ~16 the backtested frequency implies.

The mechanism: get_history(..., "1Day") returns today's partial bar while the session is open, so desired_long() was reading close.iloc[-1] as a close when it was the live intraday price. An intraday dip below the prior 20-day low flipped the signal and triggered a trend_break exit the backtester — which only ever evaluates true daily closes — can never take. Price recovered, the next tick bought back in, and the spread was paid twice.

The number that makes it unambiguous

The ten names the agent was holding — AAPL, BAC, CRM, DIS, JNJ, JPM, MA, MSFT, NVDA, V — returned an average of +16.8% over the live window. Simply holding exactly what it held would have made 16.8%. It made 0.82%. The stock selection worked; the churn ate all of it.

Fixed in 334750e: completed_bars() drops the in-progress bar before the channel read, with a regression test that reproduces the bug and asserts live/backtest signal equivalence on closed data.

The deeper failure is not the two-line bug. It is that nothing in the system was comparing live behaviour against backtested behaviour. A project whose entire thesis is "measure before you trust it" ran a different strategy than the one it validated, for three months, and the only reason it surfaced was someone writing documentation.

What was achieved on the engineering side

Reproducible edge

One strategy definition, two consumers

desired_long() is called by both the live engine and the backtester. There is no separate live implementation to drift from the tested one — the thing measured is literally the thing that runs.

Model isolation

An LLM that cannot exceed its brief

The news component has no code path that increases risk. No prompt injection, jailbreak or malformed response can open a position, raise a limit or skip a stop, because those branches do not exist.

Auditability

Decisions replayable months later

Every tick's document carries the headlines, model and prompt version behind each verdict. Production decisions can be re-scored against a future prompt — the journal is an eval corpus, not just a log.

Falsifiability

Harnesses that can say no

Both measurement tools returned unwelcome answers and both were acted on. A harness that has never overruled its author is not evidence of a good strategy; it is evidence of a decorative harness.

Two reusable patterns came out of this that are not specific to trading. The PreToolUse gate is a general answer to constraining an agent's actions: intercept the tool call, evaluate it with a pure function, deny before it reaches the server. And the counterfactual A/B is a general answer to valuing a model component that can only subtract — run both arms through one simulator and diff the outcomes rather than grading the classifier.

What the numbers do and don't support

Supported

That the daily Donchian strategy had positive expectancy net of modelled costs over this universe and window. That the original intraday strategy did not, by a margin too large to be sampling noise. That the news veto's cost is near zero. That the risk layer holds — no limit has ever been breached by a model action in either mode.

Not supported

That this makes money going forward — and this is no longer merely unproven. Over 93 days live it returned +0.82% against +5.70% for holding the same names. That window is too short and too regime-specific to condemn the strategy, but it is real out-of-sample evidence and it points the wrong way.

Also not supported: that the news filter has predictive edge — the eval hints at it but n=11 cannot establish it. That the Sharpe figure is comparable to a fund's, since it is a per-trade annualisation rather than a daily-returns Sharpe. That the strategy generalises beyond liquid US mega-caps in a bull market, which is the only regime it has been seriously tested in.

The gap between those two boxes is the honest position: this project produced good evidence about which of its own components were worth building, and weak evidence about whether the result is profitable. Those are different claims and the second one needs years of forward data, not a better backtest.

What is still wrong with it

Unhedged, because a write-up that lists only strengths is marketing.

Measurement

  • The trend edge was measured on mega-cap survivors in a bullish window — the most flattering possible sample. Real validation needs out-of-sample data, a delisted-inclusive universe, and walk-forward testing.
  • The eval's hindsight contamination is structural. No amount of compute fixes it; only forward measurement on unseen dates does.
  • Sharpe is per-trade annualised, not daily-returns. Do not compare it to published figures.

Live mechanics

  • Nothing compares live behaviour to backtested behaviour. This is the gap that let the partial-bar bug run for three months. Trade rate, holding period and exit-time-of-day are all cheap to assert against the backtest's distribution, and none of them were.
  • No resting stops. Fractional positions cannot carry brackets, so exits depend on the engine ticking. Overnight and weekend gap risk is unhedged.
  • Stale drawdown anchor after a mid-session balance change trips a false kill-switch, and recovery is manual Redis surgery.
  • No liveness assertion that the scheduler interval actually fired. The bug class that once left agents ticking only on restart would not be caught automatically today.
  • The trend engine does not cancel stale orders. The legacy LLM path does; the reconciliation was never ported across.
  • No startup reconciliation of broker positions against local expectations.
  • cancel_all_orders is exposed to the legacy agent but matches neither risk-hook matcher, so the gate never sees it. Benign today — it would be allowed anyway — but the code reads as though it is gated.

Structural

  • Single process, single account. No horizontal story, and none needed.
  • The Alpaca MCP service is currently unbuildable from upstream main.
  • Beating buy-and-hold consistently is hard. The realistic win here is similar return at materially lower drawdown and exposure, plus the framework to keep testing.

Where it goes next

The measurement work points at an obvious ordering, and it is not "add more strategies".

Verify the fix forward. The partial-bar fix is deployed but unproven. The check is mechanical: trade rate should fall toward roughly 16 actions per 93 days, exits should cluster after the close rather than mid-session, and average hold should climb toward the backtest's 51 bars. If it does not, the divergence has another cause.

Then close the remaining live-mechanics gaps. Startup reconciliation, per-tick stale-order cancellation in the trend path, a liveness assertion on the scheduler, and anchor invalidation. These are small, they are known, and every one of them is a way the system could lose money for reasons unrelated to whether the strategy is any good.

Then earn the right to trust the edge. Walk-forward testing on a universe that includes delisted names, and out-of-sample windows that include a genuine bear regime. The current numbers are a hypothesis, not a result.

Leave the news filter alone. The eval says it is worth approximately nothing, measured generously. The correct response to that is not a better prompt — it is to stop spending attention there until something changes.

The one-line summary

A project whose most valuable output was a measurement that told its author to delete the thing he had just built — twice.
